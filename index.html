Selected Research

Peer-reviewed publications

CXRG-SVLM: A parameter-efficient small vision-language model for multi-view chest X-ray report generation in resource-constrained environments

M. Fareed, J. Uddin, A. H. Sayyid, A. Ahmed, and M. A. Sattar. Scientific Reports 16, 35452 (2026).

My contribution. I helped implement 4-bit QLoRA to adapt the quantized Qwen2.5-3B language model for chest X-ray report generation.

The study used frontal and lateral chest radiographs and clinical indication text to generate report findings. On the Indiana University dataset, the model achieved 0.3527 ROUGE-L and 0.2744 METEOR. It trained 1.91% of loaded parameters and averaged 5.27 seconds per report on one GPU. The paper identifies radiologist evaluation as a next step.

A systematic review of ethical considerations of large language models in healthcare and medicine

M. Fareed, M. Fatima, J. Uddin, A. Ahmed, and M. A. Sattar. Frontiers in Digital Health (2025).

My contribution. I contributed to formal analysis, critical review, and manuscript editing.

The review synthesized 27 studies and identified recurring concerns about bias, patient safety, transparency, accountability, and privacy. We proposed a preliminary framework for responsible use of large language models in healthcare.

PEFT-mBERT: Benchmarking a Transformer-Based Encoder via Low-Rank and Activation-Based Adaptation for Resource-Efficient Punjabi Sentiment Analysis

A. Ahmed, S. Riaz, and M. Fareed (2026). First-author manuscript · Submitted · Evaluation being revisited.

I designed comparative mBERT experiments for Punjabi sentiment classification, comparing LoRA and IA³ with full fine-tuning and measuring model accuracy and computing requirements.

I am reevaluating the methods on a separate test split, with oversampling limited to the training data. I plan to report performance across random seeds alongside peak GPU memory and model size. A private GitHub repository contains code for my master’s thesis and this manuscript.

Thesis Work

Benchmarking PEFT Techniques for Low Resource Domain Adaptations in Sentiment Analysis

My master’s thesis benchmarks full fine-tuning (FFT) and six parameter-efficient adaptation methods on a pretrained transformer for sentiment classification in Urdu, Roman Urdu, and Punjabi (Hindi style). The evaluation compares classification accuracy and the proportion of trainable parameters, with particular attention to spelling variation in Roman Urdu.

I compared LoRA, AdaLoRA, prefix tuning, P-Tuning v2, prompt tuning, and IA³ with full fine-tuning. I am reviewing the data split procedure before sharing the thesis accuracy figures here.
