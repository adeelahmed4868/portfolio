Thesis Work

Benchmarking PEFT Techniques for Low Resource Domain Adaptations in Sentiment Analysis

My master’s thesis benchmarks full fine-tuning (FFT) and six parameter-efficient adaptation methods on a pretrained transformer for sentiment classification in Urdu, Roman Urdu, and Punjabi (Hindi style). The evaluation compares classification accuracy and the proportion of trainable parameters, with particular attention to spelling variation in Roman Urdu.

MethodRoman UrduUrduPunjabiTrainable parametersOther findings
Full fine-tuning94%90%90%100%Highest accuracy across all three datasets
LoRA92%85%85%About 1.06%Strongest overall parameter-efficient method
AdaLoRA———Similar to LoRACompetitive on Roman Urdu and Punjabi

IA³87%73%75%<0.02%Strongest result on Roman Urdu

Bars show reported accuracy on a 0–100% scale. A dash indicates that the abstract did not give an exact value. Ranges and qualitative findings are shown in the final column.

Full fine-tuning set the highest accuracy baseline on all three datasets. LoRA offered the strongest overall accuracy among the parameter-efficient methods, remaining within 2 to 5 percentage points of that baseline while updating about 1.06% of model parameters. AdaLoRA was also competitive on Roman Urdu and Punjabi.

Prefix Tuning showed substantial variation across datasets, reaching 81% accuracy on Punjabi and 33% on Roman Urdu. P-Tuning v2 achieved 62–64%, while Prompt Tuning achieved 52–60%. Prompt Tuning and IA³ each updated fewer than 0.02% of parameters. IA³ was strongest on Roman Urdu, indicating that parameter efficiency alone did not predict consistent performance across languages.

Research Interests

    Selected Publications

      PEFT-mBERT: Punjabi Sentiment Analysis

      First-author manuscript · Submitted

      This study evaluates LoRA and IA³ against full fine-tuning of multilingual BERT for Punjabi sentiment classification. The experimental design combines repeated runs across random seeds, 95% confidence intervals, and McNemar’s test with hardware profiling of peak GPU memory, inference latency, and model storage.

      • LoRA reached 90.17% peak accuracy versus 89.90% for full fine-tuning, while reducing storage from 681.28 MB to 10.13 MB. McNemar’s test found no statistically significant difference between their predictions (p = 0.2531).
      • IA³ reached 82.82% accuracy using 2.24 GB of GPU memory and 2.91 MB of model storage.

      My Contributions to Published Papers

      CXRG-SVLM: Chest X-ray Report Generation

      Scientific Reports · 2026

      My contribution: I contributed to the development of the vision-language model and assisted with the implementation of 4-bit QLoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation.

      What the study found: The model conditions generation of the Findings section on frontal and lateral chest radiographs and clinical indication text. Evaluation on the Indiana University dataset yielded ROUGE-L of 0.3527 and METEOR of 0.2744. The implementation trained 1.91% of loaded parameters and averaged 5.27 seconds per report on a single GPU. The authors identify radiologist evaluation as a next step.

      Ethics of Large Language Models in Healthcare

      Frontiers in Digital Health · 2025

      My contribution: My contributions included formal analysis, critical review, and manuscript editing.

      What the study found: The systematic review synthesized 27 studies and identified recurring concerns involving bias, patient safety, transparency, accountability, and privacy. The authors proposed a preliminary framework for the responsible use of large language models in healthcare.
